# Osusu — Institutional Overview
**Prepared for:** Introductory discussions with licensed banks, payment service providers and card programme partners  
**Prepared by:** Delight Ventures — Osusu project team  
**Market focus:** Sierra Leone first  
**Status:** Product development and partner discovery  
**Date:** 12 November 2026

> **Important:** This is an exploratory project overview, not an offer of financial services, licence claim, partnership announcement or request to move customer funds. Osusu is not currently presented as an authorised deposit-taker, e-money issuer, payment service provider or investment provider. Any regulated service would be delivered only after the legal structure, partner authority and required regulatory permissions are confirmed.

## 1. Executive summary
Osusu is a digital savings-management and financial-services platform being developed by Delight Ventures. Its goal is to make it easier for individuals and groups to organise savings, monitor financial activity and access selected payment services through appropriately authorised institutional partners.

The initial market is Sierra Leone, with a longer-term ambition to serve other African markets where suitable partners and regulatory permissions exist.

Osusu is seeking exploratory discussions with institutions that may be able to provide approved savings products, payment rails, account services, settlement and reconciliation support, and card issuing or processing.

## 2. Product direction
The planned customer experience is organised around five service areas:

1. **Digital Wallet experience** — customer-facing SLE/USD balance views and transaction history, subject to a partner-led account and funds model.
2. **Payments and bank services** — selected local and cross-border payments, transfers and bank connectivity through approved providers and available rails.
3. **Osusu Savings** — personal savings goals, scheduled contribution plans and group savings coordination. Interest-bearing or investment-linked products are not currently offered.
4. **Dash Card** — potential virtual and physical card experience through an authorised issuer and programme manager.
5. **Financial Management** — statements, savings progress, spending summaries, notifications and financial reporting.

Supporting customer experience features include account security, identity-verification workflows, receipts, transaction status transparency, reminders, support and dispute escalation.

## 3. Current development status
Osusu is in product development and partner discovery. The project has implemented parts of the customer interface, account overview, savings-plan setup, savings schedule preferences, notification inbox and draft group-circle coordination.

The current implementation does **not** mean that live financial services are available. In particular:
- no customer funds are held or pooled by Osusu;
- live deposits, withdrawals and external payment execution are not enabled;
- savings contribution schedules do not automatically debit a wallet;
- no interest rate, yield or guaranteed return is configured or advertised;
- group circles do not collect pooled contributions or make rotation payouts;
- no card is issued through Osusu at this stage.

Any displayed sample values or test activity must be treated as demonstration data, not customer funds or financial performance.

## 4. Partnership model under consideration
The model to explore is a partner-backed arrangement in which:
- a suitably authorised institution provides the regulated account, deposit/e-money, payment, investment or card service within its licence and approved product terms;
- the partner's legal entity and customer-funds responsibilities are clearly identified;
- Osusu provides agreed customer-facing technology and support functions within a documented and legally reviewed scope;
- customer instructions, funds movement, settlement and reconciliation use partner-approved accounts and rails;
- customer disclosures clearly explain the product provider, fees, risks, complaints route and any applicable protection or exclusions.

The exact structure is open to discussion and requires Sierra Leone legal and regulatory review. Osusu does not assume that a bank's licence automatically covers Osusu, or that an institution's participation in a payment network gives Osusu direct access to that network.

## 5. Areas where we would welcome a partner discussion
Depending on the institution's licence, products and capabilities, we would like to understand:

- **Savings products:** whether an approved savings product can be made available to Osusu customers, who holds the funds, eligibility, terms, rates (if any), disclosures and withdrawal conditions.
- **Payments:** supported local payment rails, bank transfers, collection/disbursement options, settlement timing, reversals, limits and fees.
- **Account services:** whether account opening, account linking or balance/transaction information can be supported through approved APIs or other secure channels.
- **Technology:** API documentation, sandbox access, authentication, signed webhooks, idempotency, status enquiries, reconciliation files and technical support.
- **Compliance and operations:** KYC/AML allocation, sanctions screening, transaction monitoring, complaints, disputes, fraud handling, incident escalation and record retention.
- **Cards:** whether an authorised issuer or programme manager can support a future card programme, subject to separate assessment.

## 6. Questions we would like to explore
1. Which of these services can your institution support under its current authorisation?
2. What legal and regulatory approvals would be required for the proposed partner/technology arrangement?
3. Which entity would contract with the customer and legally hold or safeguard funds?
4. What customer due-diligence and ongoing monitoring responsibilities would apply to Osusu?
5. What integration options, sandbox environments and technical documentation are available?
6. What settlement, reconciliation, reversal and dispute processes are supported?
7. What are the commercial requirements, fees, minimum volumes, reserves and service levels?
8. What documentation and governance approvals would be required before a controlled pilot?

## 7. Principles for a responsible partnership
Osusu intends to work only with institutions whose legal identity, current authorisation and permitted activities can be independently verified. Before any live pilot, the parties would need documented agreement on responsibilities, custody/safeguarding, compliance, customer disclosures, data protection, security, operational resilience, complaints, audit rights, liability, reconciliation and exit arrangements.

No product launch or customer marketing claim will be based solely on an introductory discussion or an unverified public statement.
